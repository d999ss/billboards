### 1 · How the money actually works
A billboard is a small cash-flow machine: contracted ad revenue in, thin operating cost out, the rest is yours because you own the dirt and the structure. Base case = one premium I-15 static 2-face, owned land: $72k gross ad rev − $12k opex − $0 ground rent = **$60k net operating cash flow (≈83% of gross)**. Owning the land removes ground rent — the single biggest line every other operator pays — so margin runs ~70–85% of gross.

### 2 · Three scenarios

| Scenario | Gross/yr | Opex/yr | NOI/yr | Multiple | Price | Cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small static — secondary hwy, 2-face | $36k | ($5k) | $31k | 10× | $310k | 10.0% |

The value-creation event is the digital conversion: ~$1.0M all-in re-rates to ~$3.0M at the same multiple — only if local sign code permits digital (restrictive in UT; this is the make-or-break diligence item).

### 3 · Capital stack (stabilized digital case, $3.0M value, $270k NOI)
Senior debt 60% · $1.8M @ 8.5% · 15-yr amort → ≈$212k/yr DS. Seller note ~15% (retiring-operator paper). Cash equity balance. DSCR = 270 ÷ 212 = 1.27 ✓. Cash-on-cash ≈ (270−212) ÷ ~$1.2M ≈ 4.8% + amortization + appreciation + re-rate. At ~8.5% debt vs a ~9% cap, leverage is roughly neutral on static — the return is the unlevered ~9% + 3% escalators + the digital option + terminal re-rate.

### 4 · Scaling ladder
Sub-$1M cash flow trades at 9–12×; cross $1M aggregate NOI and the whole book re-rates to 10–13×. 1 board (~$31–60k NOI, 10×) → 3–5 boards (~$0.3M, 11×) → +1 digital (~$0.6M, 11×) → $1M+ NOI book re-rates to 10–13× → ≈$10–13M exit to a Reagan / Lamar / OUTFRONT roll-up. Minimum sane entry: one premium board, ~$250k equity. First real block: 3–5 static or one digital, ~$0.75–1.5M equity.

### 5 · The path in
Source via independent operators (UDOT OAC inventory) + a billboard broker → verify the permit is transferable and title conveys → underwrite the documented rent roll at 9–12× → finance (mostly equity/seller note on static) → close, transfer permit within 90 days, operate, then pursue digital conversion.

**Acquisition checklist:** valid transferable UDOT OAC off-premise permit (not "Official"; check renewal + grandfathered status) · fee title conveys (not a ground lease) · in-place advertiser contracts + occupancy + rate card + UDOT AADT · local digital eligibility · structure age/wind rating/condition/access · price discipline 9–12× verified net cash flow, walk if income undocumented.

## PART B — The three reframes (apply before this goes to an intro audience)

The numbers are sound. The risk is what a newcomer *does* after reading.

### Reframe 1 — Lead with the moat, not the waterfall
The one fact that makes the asset class real *and* explains the empty marketplace: **in regulated states the off-premise permit supply is legally fixed and grandfathered — you cannot build one, only buy one.** That sentence is the thesis. Move it from the last paragraph to the first screen.

### Reframe 2 — Not a menu: one base case + one lottery ticket
Static ≈ 9% unlevered is the real, repeatable outcome. Digital is a low-probability, permit-gated 3–5× re-rate. Equal visual weight makes a sharp investor say "you're selling the exception." Probability-discount the digital row, or split: *what you actually get* vs *the option you're paying for*.

### Reframe 3 — Keep and elevate the leverage-honesty line
"At 8.5% vs a 9% cap, leverage is roughly neutral." Almost no pitch admits this. It is the highest-credibility sentence in the document — pull it up; readers trust everything else *because* of it.

### Unmodeled cost to name explicitly
The re-rate is real; the path to $1M aggregate NOI is not free. 15–30 static boards, years of off-market sourcing, against majors holding ROFR in their markets. What's left for an independent assembler is, by selection, what the majors passed on. Model assembly time and adverse selection, not just the terminal multiple.
