A small cash-flow machine, hiding behind a permit you can't print.

Compiled 2026·05·18 — Illustrative underwriting ranges, not quotes

A billboard is a small cash-flow machine: contracted ad revenue in, thin operating cost out, and the rest is yours — because you own the dirt and the structure.

The fact that makes this real, and explains why nothing is listed for sale: in regulated states the off-premise permit supply is legally fixed and grandfathered. You cannot build one. You can only buy one — and they move off-market.
01How the money actually works

Base case: one premium I-15 static two-face on land you own. Owning the land removes ground rent — the single biggest line every other operator pays — so margin runs 70–85% of gross.

02What you'd actually buy

Read this as one base case plus one lottery ticket — not a menu. Static at ~9% unlevered is the real, repeatable outcome. The digital conversion is a low-probability, permit-gated re-rate. It is where the money is made, and the diligence item that makes or breaks every deal.

The honest part: at ~8.5% debt against a ~9% cap, leverage is roughly neutral on static. DSCR = 270 ÷ 212 = 1.27. Cash-on-cash ≈ 4.8% plus amortization, escalators and the re-rate. Don't expect levered IRR fireworks on a single static board — the alpha is conversion and consolidation, not financial engineering.

04How much to buy

There is a structural arbitrage: sub-$1M cash flow trades at 9–12×; cross $1M aggregate NOI and the whole book re-rates to 10–13×. Assembling boards isn't additive — clearing the threshold marks up everything you already own by one to two turns.

1 board~$31–60k NOI · 10×
3–5 boards~$0.3M NOI · 11×
+1 digital~$0.6M NOI · 11×
$1M+ book10–13× · ≈$10–13M exit

The unmodeled cost: reaching $1M aggregate NOI in static boards is 15–30 boards and years of off-market sourcing against majors holding right-of-first-refusal in their markets. What's left for an independent assembler is, by selection, what the majors passed on. Model assembly time and adverse selection — not just the terminal multiple.

05The path in

Source via independent operators on the UDOT OAC inventory plus a billboard broker → verify the permit is transferable and fee title conveys → underwrite the documented rent roll at 9–12× → finance (mostly equity / seller note on static) → close, transfer the permit within 90 days, operate, then pursue digital conversion.

  • Permit. Valid, transferable UDOT OAC off-premise permit — not "Official." Check renewal date and grandfathered status.
  • Title. Fee title to the parcel conveys, not merely a ground lease.
  • Income. In-place advertiser contracts, occupancy, rate card, UDOT AADT traffic counts.
  • Upside. Local sign-code eligibility for digital — verify first, it is the value lever.
  • Discipline. 9–12× verified net cash flow. Walk if the income isn't documented.

Bottom line. The cash flow is real but modest on a single static board, and bank leverage barely helps at today's rates — it's an equity / seller-note entry. The money is made on two moves: (1) digital conversion (~3–5× on permitted sites) and (2) assembling past $1M aggregate NOI to trigger the re-rate, then selling the book to Reagan / Lamar / OUTFRONT. In Utah you can't build the permit — you buy it, off-market.
